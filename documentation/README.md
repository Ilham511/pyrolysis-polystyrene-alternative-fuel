# Project Documentation

## Research Objective

This project investigates the conversion of Polystyrene (PS) plastic waste into liquid alternative fuel through pyrolysis using the PITARA (Multi-Stage Tubular Reactor).

## Materials and Equipment

- Polystyrene (PS) plastic waste
- PITARA Multi-Stage Tubular Reactor
- Condenser
- Bentonite adsorbent
- LPG and charcoal as heating sources

## Experimental Parameters

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Feedstock | Polystyrene (PS) |
| Feedstock mass | 4.5 kg |
| Particle size | 10 × 10 mm |
| Reactor diameter | 28 cm |
| Reactor height | 30 cm |
| Temperature | 250°C, 300°C, 350°C |
| Process duration | 180 minutes |
| Process condition | Anaerobic |
| Adsorbent | Bentonite |

## Experimental Procedure

1. Polystyrene waste was washed and dried.
2. The material was reduced to approximately 10 × 10 mm.
3. The prepared material was placed into the PITARA reactor.
4. Pyrolysis was conducted at 250°C, 300°C, and 350°C.
5. Pyrolysis vapor was condensed into liquid pyrolysis oil.
6. The resulting oil was treated with bentonite.
7. The treated oil was characterized based on density, kinematic viscosity, and flash point.

## Main Results

The highest oil volume was obtained at 300°C, reaching 6,601 mL.

The kinematic viscosity ranged from 0.795 to 0.939 mm²/s, while the flash point ranged from 25.0°C to 32.3°C.

The experimental results indicate that pyrolysis temperature affects the quantity and physicochemical characteristics of the resulting pyrolysis oil.
